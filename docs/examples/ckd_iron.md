# Prevalence of Iron Deficiency Among US Adults With CKD: A Cross-Sectional Analysis of NHANES 2017-March 2020 pre-pandemic

**Key Points**

- **Question:** Among noninstitutionalized US participants in NHANES 2017-March 2020 pre-pandemic (Age >=18 years; CKD (eGFR <60 or UACR >=30); Not on dialysis; Measured serum ferritin, transferrin saturation), what is the survey-weighted prevalence of iron deficiency (non-dialysis CKD: TSAT <20% and ferritin <100 ng/mL (hemodialysis: ferritin <200 ng/mL)), overall and by sex, age group, and race and ethnicity?
- **Findings:** In this cross-sectional study of 1,791 participants (weighted to represent approximately 49.5 million US residents; 49.2% were female (weighted) and 57.4% were aged 65 years or older), the weighted prevalence of iron deficiency was 8.8% (95% CI, 7.1%-10.8%), corresponding to approximately 4.3 million people.
- **Meaning:** Iron deficiency affects a substantial share of this population under current guideline-based definitions; case definitions materially change prevalence, so the definition should be reported explicitly.

## Introduction

Iron deficiency is a common, treatable condition whose estimated burden depends on the case definition. Contemporary nationally representative estimates aligned with current guideline thresholds are needed. National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES) data permit design-based estimation that generalizes to the noninstitutionalized US population. We aimed to estimate the nationally representative prevalence of iron deficiency and its distribution across demographic subgroups.

## Results

The analytic sample included 1,791 participants (weighted to represent approximately 49.5 million US residents; 49.2% were female (weighted) and 57.4% were aged 65 years or older). Overall, the weighted prevalence of iron deficiency was 8.8% (95% CI, 7.1%-10.8%), corresponding to approximately 4.3 million people. Prevalence ranged from 5.0% (sex: Male) to 14.7% (age group: 18-39). 4 of 4 sensitivity analyses agree in direction (associations) or magnitude (prevalence).

## Discussion

In this nationally representative cross-sectional analysis, the weighted prevalence of iron deficiency was 8.8% (95% CI, 7.1%-10.8%), corresponding to approximately 4.3 million people. Strengths include a prespecified, investigator-approved protocol; guideline-versioned, citable case definitions; design-based variance estimation with appropriate examination weights; NCHS reliability standards; multiple prespecified sensitivity analyses; and a fully reproducible, provenance-tracked pipeline. Limitations: Cross-sectional design: temporality cannot be established and associations must not be interpreted causally. Single measurement of laboratory values; within-person variability and chronicity cannot be assessed. Residual confounding by unmeasured or imperfectly measured factors (e.g., diet, medications, menstrual blood loss). Self-reported comorbidities and behaviours are subject to recall and social-desirability bias. NHANES excludes institutionalized and active-duty military populations. Operational definition (KDIGO 2026 systemic ID): Terminology ('systemic iron deficiency' / 'iron-restricted erythropoiesis') reported from the ERBP commentary on KDIGO 2026; confirm wording against the guideline PDF.
