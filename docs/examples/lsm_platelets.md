# Association of Liver Stiffness With Thrombocytopenia Among US Adults: NHANES 2017-March 2020 pre-pandemic

**Key Points**

- **Question:** Among noninstitutionalized US participants in NHANES 2017-March 2020 pre-pandemic (Age >=18 years; Reliable liver elastography (complete exam, IQR/median <30%)), is greater liver stiffness (VCTE median) (per 5 kPa) associated with a higher prevalence of thrombocytopenia - platelets <150 x10^9/L, after adjustment for age, sex, race and ethnicity, income-to-poverty ratio, body mass index, diabetes (self-report, medication, or HbA1c >=6.5%), and smoking status?
- **Findings:** In this cross-sectional study of 5,827 participants (weighted to represent approximately 167.5 million US residents; 49.9% were female (weighted) and 29.2% were aged 65 years or older), liver stiffness (VCTE median) per 5 kPa was associated with Thrombocytopenia - platelets <150 x10^9/L (PR, 1.65 (95% CI, 1.60-1.71)) after adjustment for age, sex, race and ethnicity, income-to-poverty ratio, body mass index, diabetes (self-report, medication, or HbA1c >=6.5%), smoking status.
- **Meaning:** This association is cross-sectional and may reflect residual confounding or reverse causation; prospective studies are needed to establish temporality.

## Discussion

In this nationally representative cross-sectional analysis, liver stiffness (VCTE median) per 5 kPa was associated with Thrombocytopenia - platelets <150 x10^9/L (PR, 1.65 (95% CI, 1.60-1.71)) after adjustment for age, sex, race and ethnicity, income-to-poverty ratio, body mass index, diabetes (self-report, medication, or HbA1c >=6.5%), smoking status. Strengths include a prespecified, investigator-approved protocol; guideline-versioned, citable case definitions; design-based variance estimation with appropriate examination weights; NCHS reliability standards; multiple prespecified sensitivity analyses; and a fully reproducible, provenance-tracked pipeline. Limitations: Cross-sectional design: temporality cannot be established and associations must not be interpreted causally. Single measurement of laboratory values; within-person variability and chronicity cannot be assessed. Residual confounding by unmeasured or imperfectly measured factors (e.g., diet, medications, menstrual blood loss). Self-reported comorbidities and behaviours are subject to recall and social-desirability bias. NHANES excludes institutionalized and active-duty military populations.
