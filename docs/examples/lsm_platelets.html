Introduction

Whether liver stiffness (VCTE median) is associated with thrombocytopenia - platelets <150 x10^9/L beyond established risk factors is uncertain in nationally representative data. National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES) data permit design-based estimation that generalizes to the noninstitutionalized US population. We aimed to evaluate whether liver stiffness (VCTE median) is associated with thrombocytopenia - platelets <150 x10^9/L among US adults.

Methods

Data Source

NHANES is a continuous, nationally representative survey of the US civilian noninstitutionalized population using a stratified, multistage probability design, with household interviews and standardized physical examinations and laboratory testing in mobile examination centers (MECs). We used NHANES 2017-March 2020 pre-pandemic. NHANES protocols were approved by the National Center for Health Statistics Research Ethics Review Board and participants provided written informed consent; this analysis of deidentified public-use data did not require additional institutional review board review. We followed the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) reporting guideline.

Statistical Analysis

Binary outcome: survey-weighted quasi-Poisson regression with log link estimates prevalence ratios (PRs). PRs are preferred to odds ratios for cross-sectional data because ORs exaggerate associations when the outcome is common (>10%); the quasi-Poisson variance is robust under the design-based (Taylor linearization) estimator. Logistic regression is run as a sensitivity analysis. All analyses incorporated the WTMECPRP sample weight (mec component), masked variance pseudo-strata and pseudo-PSUs (Taylor series linearization; 15 strata, 33 PSUs, design df = 18); subpopulations were analyzed with domain (subset) estimation to preserve the full design. Proportions use Korn-Graubard 95% CIs and were evaluated against the NCHS Data Presentation Standards for Proportions. Regression inference used t distributions with design degrees of freedom. Covariates were selected as plausible common causes of liver stiffness (VCTE median) and thrombocytopenia - platelets <150 x10^9/L (minimal confounder set from the domain library): Age, Sex, Race and ethnicity, Income-to-poverty ratio, Body mass index, Diabetes (self-report, medication, or HbA1c ≥6.5%), and Smoking status. Variables on the causal pathway or sharing laboratory components with the outcome/exposure are excluded to avoid over-adjustment. Effect modification was assessed with Wald F tests of product terms (Sex, Age group). Complete-case primary analysis within the design; multiple imputation by chained equations as sensitivity analysis when covariates are missing. Prespecified sensitivity analyses: Exclude heavy alcohol (>2 drinks/day men, >1 women); Exclude self-reported hepatitis B or C; Logistic regression (odds ratio); Multiple imputation of missing covariates (m = 10); Exposure in weighted quartiles (Q4 vs Q1, P for trend); Unweighted analysis (design ignored). E-values quantified the minimum strength of unmeasured confounding needed to explain away the primary association. Two-sided P < .05 denoted significance. Analyses used R version 4.3.3 (2024-02-29) with survey 4.2.1, mice 3.16.0 and ggplot2 3.4.4, orchestrated by Q2E.

Results

The analytic sample included 5,827 participants (weighted to represent approximately 167.5 million US residents; 49.9% were female (weighted) and 29.2% were aged 65 years or older). Overall, liver stiffness (VCTE median) per 5 kPa was associated with Thrombocytopenia - platelets <150 x10^9/L (PR, 1.65 (95% CI, 1.60-1.71)) after adjustment for age, sex, race and ethnicity, income-to-poverty ratio, body mass index, diabetes (self-report, medication, or HbA1c ≥6.5%), smoking status. 6 of 6 sensitivity analyses agree in direction (associations) or magnitude (prevalence).

The E-value for the primary estimate was 2.69 (CI limit 2.58); Prevalence ratio used directly.

Effect modification by sex: P = .09 for interaction (Male: 1.61 (1.53-1.69); Female: 1.73 (1.62-1.84)).

Effect modification by age group: P = .82 for interaction (18-39: 1.68 (1.54-1.83); 40-64: 1.66 (1.55-1.78); 65+: 1.66 (1.55-1.76)).

Discussion

In this nationally representative cross-sectional analysis, liver stiffness (VCTE median) per 5 kPa was associated with Thrombocytopenia - platelets <150 x10^9/L (PR, 1.65 (95% CI, 1.60-1.71)) after adjustment for age, sex, race and ethnicity, income-to-poverty ratio, body mass index, diabetes (self-report, medication, or HbA1c ≥6.5%), smoking status. Strengths include a prespecified, investigator-approved protocol; guideline-versioned, citable case definitions; design-based variance estimation with appropriate examination weights; NCHS reliability standards; multiple prespecified sensitivity analyses; and a fully reproducible, provenance-tracked pipeline. Limitations: Cross-sectional design: temporality cannot be established and associations must not be interpreted causally. Single measurement of laboratory values; within-person variability and chronicity cannot be assessed. Residual confounding by unmeasured or imperfectly measured factors (e.g., diet, medications, menstrual blood loss). Self-reported comorbidities and behaviours are subject to recall and social-desirability bias. NHANES excludes institutionalized and active-duty military populations.
