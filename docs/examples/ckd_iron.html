Introduction

Iron deficiency is a common, treatable condition whose estimated burden depends on the case definition. Contemporary nationally representative estimates aligned with current guideline thresholds are needed. National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES) data permit design-based estimation that generalizes to the noninstitutionalized US population. We aimed to estimate the nationally representative prevalence of iron deficiency and its distribution across demographic subgroups.

Methods

Data Source

NHANES is a continuous, nationally representative survey of the US civilian noninstitutionalized population using a stratified, multistage probability design, with household interviews and standardized physical examinations and laboratory testing in mobile examination centers (MECs). We used NHANES 2017-March 2020 pre-pandemic. NHANES protocols were approved by the National Center for Health Statistics Research Ethics Review Board and participants provided written informed consent; this analysis of deidentified public-use data did not require additional institutional review board review. We followed the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) reporting guideline.

Statistical Analysis

Prevalence estimand: survey-weighted proportions with Korn-Graubard (beta) 95% CIs, overall and by subgroup, assessed against NCHS presentation standards; population counts from weighted totals. Adjusted correlates are estimated with survey-weighted quasi-Poisson regression (prevalence ratios). All analyses incorporated the WTMECPRP sample weight (mec component), masked variance pseudo-strata and pseudo-PSUs (Taylor series linearization; 15 strata, 33 PSUs, design df = 18); subpopulations were analyzed with domain (subset) estimation to preserve the full design. Proportions use Korn-Graubard 95% CIs and were evaluated against the NCHS Data Presentation Standards for Proportions. Regression inference used t distributions with design degrees of freedom. Correlates model includes Age, Sex, Race and ethnicity, Income-to-poverty ratio, Body mass index, and Systemic inflammation (hsCRP >5 mg/L) to describe independent associations with systemic iron deficiency in non-dialysis CKD - KDIGO 2026 (TSAT <20% and ferritin <100 ng/mL); these are descriptive, not causal, contrasts. Variables on the causal pathway or sharing laboratory components with the outcome/exposure are excluded to avoid over-adjustment. Complete-case primary analysis within the design; multiple imputation by chained equations as sensitivity analysis when covariates are missing. Prespecified sensitivity analyses: Alternative outcome definition: KDIGO 2026 IRE; Alternative outcome definition: KDIGO 2026 iron-eligible; Alternative outcome definition: KDIGO 2012; Exclude hsCRP >5 mg/L (inflammation raises ferritin); Exclude participants treated for anemia; Exclude pregnant participants; Unweighted analysis (design ignored). Two-sided P < .05 denoted significance. Analyses used R version 4.3.3 (2024-02-29) with survey 4.2.1, mice 3.16.0 and ggplot2 3.4.4, orchestrated by Q2E.

Results

The analytic sample included 1,791 participants (weighted to represent approximately 49.5 million US residents; 49.2% were female (weighted) and 57.4% were aged 65 years or older). Overall, the weighted prevalence of iron deficiency was 8.8% (95% CI, 7.1%-10.8%), corresponding to approximately 4.3 million people. Prevalence ranged from 5.0% (sex: Male) to 14.7% (age group: 18-39). 4 of 4 sensitivity analyses agree in direction (associations) or magnitude (prevalence).

Discussion

In this nationally representative cross-sectional analysis, the weighted prevalence of iron deficiency was 8.8% (95% CI, 7.1%-10.8%), corresponding to approximately 4.3 million people. Strengths include a prespecified, investigator-approved protocol; guideline-versioned, citable case definitions; design-based variance estimation with appropriate examination weights; NCHS reliability standards; multiple prespecified sensitivity analyses; and a fully reproducible, provenance-tracked pipeline. Limitations: Cross-sectional design: temporality cannot be established and associations must not be interpreted causally. Single measurement of laboratory values; within-person variability and chronicity cannot be assessed. Residual confounding by unmeasured or imperfectly measured factors (e.g., diet, medications, menstrual blood loss). Self-reported comorbidities and behaviours are subject to recall and social-desirability bias. NHANES excludes institutionalized and active-duty military populations. Operational definition (KDIGO 2026 systemic ID): Terminology ('systemic iron deficiency' / 'iron-restricted erythropoiesis') reported from the ERBP commentary on KDIGO 2026; confirm wording against the guideline PDF.

Limitations

  • Cross-sectional design: temporality cannot be established and associations must not be interpreted causally.
  • Single measurement of laboratory values; within-person variability and chronicity cannot be assessed.
  • Residual confounding by unmeasured or imperfectly measured factors (e.g., diet, medications, menstrual blood loss).
  • Self-reported comorbidities and behaviours are subject to recall and social-desirability bias.
  • NHANES excludes institutionalized and active-duty military populations.
  • Operational definition (KDIGO 2026 systemic ID): Terminology ('systemic iron deficiency' / 'iron-restricted erythropoiesis') reported from the ERBP commentary on KDIGO 2026; confirm wording against the guideline PDF.
  • Operational definition (KDIGO 2026 systemic ID): CKD defined from a single NHANES visit (eGFR <60 or UACR ≥30); chronicity (>3 months) cannot be confirmed.
