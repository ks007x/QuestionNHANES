# Association of Abdominal Obesity (Waist Circumference) With Glycemic Control (HbA1c) Among US Adults: NHANES 2017-March 2020 pre-pandemic

**Key Points**

- **Question:** Among noninstitutionalized US participants in NHANES 2017-March 2020 pre-pandemic (Age >=18 years; excluding pregnant at examination), is greater waist circumference (per 10 cm) associated with higher HbA1c, after adjustment for age, sex, race and ethnicity, income-to-poverty ratio, educational attainment, smoking status, and body mass index?
- **Findings:** In this cross-sectional study of 7,988 participants (weighted to represent approximately 226.3 million US residents; 51.1% were female (weighted) and 20.1% were aged 65 years or older), waist circumference per 10 cm was associated with HbA1c (β, 0.15 (95% CI, 0.10-0.21)) after adjustment for age, sex, race and ethnicity, income-to-poverty ratio, educational attainment, smoking status, body mass index.
- **Meaning:** This association is cross-sectional and may reflect residual confounding or reverse causation; prospective studies are needed to establish temporality.

## Discussion

In this nationally representative cross-sectional analysis, waist circumference per 10 cm was associated with HbA1c (β, 0.15 (95% CI, 0.10-0.21)) after adjustment for age, sex, race and ethnicity, income-to-poverty ratio, educational attainment, smoking status, body mass index. Strengths include a prespecified, investigator-approved protocol; guideline-versioned, citable case definitions; design-based variance estimation with appropriate examination weights; NCHS reliability standards; multiple prespecified sensitivity analyses; and a fully reproducible, provenance-tracked pipeline. Limitations: Cross-sectional design: temporality cannot be established and associations must not be interpreted causally. Single measurement of laboratory values; within-person variability and chronicity cannot be assessed. Residual confounding by unmeasured or imperfectly measured factors (e.g., diet, medications, menstrual blood loss). Self-reported comorbidities and behaviours are subject to recall and social-desirability bias. NHANES excludes institutionalized and active-duty military populations.

## Methods summary

- **Design:** cross-sectional analysis of the National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES), 2017-March 2020 pre-pandemic.
- **Population:** Age >=18 years; excluding pregnant at examination (analytic n = 6,610).
- **Outcome:** HbA1c.
- **Exposure:** Waist circumference.
- **Comparator:** per-unit contrast along the exposure (lower vs higher values); quartiles as sensitivity.
- **Covariates:** Age, Sex, Race and ethnicity, Income-to-poverty ratio, Educational attainment, Smoking status, Body mass index.
- **Descriptive:** survey-weighted Table 1 by abdominal obesity - waist >102 cm (men) / >88 cm (women); weighted prevalence with 95% CIs.
- **Model:** mean differences (survey-weighted linear regression); sequential models: Unadjusted, Adjusted for age, sex, and race and ethnicity, Fully adjusted (primary).
- **Effect modification:** interaction tests and stratified estimates by sex, age group.
- **Survey design:** Taylor linearisation with strata and PSUs; weight WTMECPRP; design df = 25.
- **Sensitivity analyses:** 8 prespecified.
  - Binary outcome: Elevated HbA1c (>=6.5%) (PR)
  - Exclude participants on glucose-lowering therapy
  - Exclude anemia (HbA1c unreliable)
  - Multiple imputation of missing covariates (m = 10)
  - Exposure in weighted quartiles (Q4 vs Q1, P for trend)
  - Alternative exposure: Abdominal obesity - waist >102 cm (men) / >88 cm (women)
  - Alternative exposure: Central adiposity - waist-to-height ratio >=0.5
  - Unweighted analysis (design ignored)
- **Missing data:** complete-case primary analysis; multiple imputation as sensitivity.
- **Reporting:** STROBE; plan pre-registered by hash before data access.
- **Sample size:** Alpha 0.05 (two-sided), power 80%, target 0.2 SD, DEFF 1.8: Minimum detectable difference estimated from the run's design-based standard errors. After the run: Waist circumference (per 10 cm): minimum detectable difference 0.0786 (outcome units).

## Detected limitations

- **Temporality** (major): Cross-sectional: exposure and outcome are measured at the same visit, so the direction of association cannot be established.
- **Confounding** (major): Residual and unmeasured confounding cannot be excluded in an observational analysis.
- **Measurement** (moderate): Laboratory values come from a single blood or urine sample; transient abnormalities may be misclassified as persistent.
- **Missing data** (minor): Complete-case loss: 17.3% of the analytic sample (1378) lack >=1 covariate. Multiple-imputation sensitivity analysis recommended.
