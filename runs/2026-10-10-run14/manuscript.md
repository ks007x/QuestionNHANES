# Prevalence of Functional Iron Deficiency Among US Adults: A Cross-Sectional Analysis of NHANES 2017-March 2020 pre-pandemic

**Key Points**

- **Question:** Among noninstitutionalized US participants in NHANES 2017-March 2020 pre-pandemic (Age >=18 years; Measured serum ferritin, transferrin saturation), what is the survey-weighted prevalence of functional iron deficiency (serum ferritin >=30 ng/mL with transferrin saturation <20%), overall and by sex, age group, and race and ethnicity?
- **Findings:** In this cross-sectional study of 8,212 participants (weighted to represent approximately 231.3 million US residents; 51.7% were female (weighted) and 20.4% were aged 65 years or older), the weighted prevalence of functional iron deficiency was 15.4% (95% CI, 14.4%-16.5%), corresponding to approximately 35.7 million people.
- **Meaning:** Functional iron deficiency affects a substantial share of this population under current guideline-based definitions; case definitions materially change prevalence, so the definition should be reported explicitly.

## Abstract

**Importance:** Functional iron deficiency is a common, treatable condition whose estimated burden depends on the case definition. Contemporary nationally representative estimates aligned with current guideline thresholds are needed.

**Objective:** To estimate the nationally representative prevalence of functional iron deficiency and its distribution across demographic subgroups.

**Design, Setting, and Participants:** This cross-sectional study used NHANES 2017-March 2020 pre-pandemic data. Eligible participants: Age >=18 years; Measured serum ferritin, transferrin saturation. Data were analyzed on October 10, 2026.

**Exposures:** Demographic and clinical subgroups (Sex, Age group, Race and ethnicity).

**Main Outcomes and Measures:** Functional iron deficiency - ferritin >=30 ng/mL with TSAT <20%: Serum ferritin >=30 ng/mL with transferrin saturation <20%. (Tawfik YMK, Billingsley H, Bhatt AS, et al. Absolute and Functional Iron Deficiency in the US, 2017-2020. JAMA Netw Open. 2024;7(9):e2433126.)

**Conclusions and Relevance:** In this cross-sectional study of US adults, the weighted prevalence of functional iron deficiency was 15.4% (95% CI, 14.4%-16.5%), corresponding to approximately 35.7 million people. Functional iron deficiency affects a substantial share of this population under current guideline-based definitions; case definitions materially change prevalence, so the definition should be reported explicitly.

## Introduction

Functional iron deficiency is a common, treatable condition whose estimated burden depends on the case definition. Contemporary nationally representative estimates aligned with current guideline thresholds are needed. National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES) data permit design-based estimation that generalizes to the noninstitutionalized US population. We aimed to estimate the nationally representative prevalence of functional iron deficiency and its distribution across demographic subgroups.

## Results

The analytic sample included 8,212 participants (weighted to represent approximately 231.3 million US residents; 51.7% were female (weighted) and 20.4% were aged 65 years or older). Overall, the weighted prevalence of functional iron deficiency was 15.4% (95% CI, 14.4%-16.5%), corresponding to approximately 35.7 million people. Prevalence ranged from 11.8% (sex: Male) to 20.5% (race and ethnicity: Other or multiracial). 4 of 4 sensitivity analyses agree in direction (associations) or magnitude (prevalence). The estimate was consistent with the published estimate of 15% (Tawfik et al. 2024, US adults >=18 y, NHANES 2017-March 2020).

## Discussion

In this nationally representative cross-sectional analysis, the weighted prevalence of functional iron deficiency was 15.4% (95% CI, 14.4%-16.5%), corresponding to approximately 35.7 million people. The estimate was consistent with the published estimate of 15% (Tawfik et al. 2024, US adults >=18 y, NHANES 2017-March 2020). Strengths include a prespecified, investigator-approved protocol; guideline-versioned, citable case definitions; design-based variance estimation with appropriate examination weights; NCHS reliability standards; multiple prespecified sensitivity analyses; and a fully reproducible, provenance-tracked pipeline. Limitations: Cross-sectional design: temporality cannot be established and associations must not be interpreted causally. Single measurement of laboratory values; within-person variability and chronicity cannot be assessed. Residual confounding by unmeasured or imperfectly measured factors (e.g., diet, medications, menstrual blood loss). Self-reported comorbidities and behaviours are subject to recall and social-desirability bias. NHANES excludes institutionalized and active-duty military populations. Operational definition (Ferritin >=30 & TSAT <20%): TSAT uses the NHANES-reported value (LBDPCT); if absent it is computed as 100 x serum iron / TIBC.

## Limitations
