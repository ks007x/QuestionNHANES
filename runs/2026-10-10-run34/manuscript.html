Introduction

Whether hypertension - ACC/AHA 2017 (BP ≥130/80 mm hg or antihypertensive use) is associated with CKD - eGFR <60 or UACR ≥30 mg/g (single measurement) beyond established risk factors is uncertain in nationally representative data. National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES) data permit design-based estimation that generalizes to the noninstitutionalized US population. We aimed to evaluate whether hypertension - ACC/AHA 2017 (BP ≥130/80 mm hg or antihypertensive use) is associated with CKD - eGFR <60 or UACR ≥30 mg/g (single measurement) among US adults.

Methods

Data Source

NHANES is a continuous, nationally representative survey of the US civilian noninstitutionalized population using a stratified, multistage probability design, with household interviews and standardized physical examinations and laboratory testing in mobile examination centers (MECs). We used NHANES 2017-March 2020 pre-pandemic. NHANES protocols were approved by the National Center for Health Statistics Research Ethics Review Board and participants provided written informed consent; this analysis of deidentified public-use data did not require additional institutional review board review. We followed the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) reporting guideline.

Covariates

Statistical Analysis

Binary outcome: survey-weighted quasi-Poisson regression with log link estimates prevalence ratios (PRs). PRs are preferred to odds ratios for cross-sectional data because ORs exaggerate associations when the outcome is common (>10%); the quasi-Poisson variance is robust under the design-based (Taylor linearization) estimator. Logistic regression is run as a sensitivity analysis. All analyses incorporated the WTMECPRP sample weight (mec component), masked variance pseudo-strata and pseudo-PSUs (Taylor series linearization; 24 strata, 49 PSUs, design df = 25); subpopulations were analyzed with domain (subset) estimation to preserve the full design. Proportions use Korn-Graubard 95% CIs and were evaluated against the NCHS Data Presentation Standards for Proportions. Regression inference used t distributions with design degrees of freedom. Covariates were selected as plausible common causes of hypertension - ACC/AHA 2017 (BP ≥130/80 mm hg or antihypertensive use) and CKD - eGFR <60 or UACR ≥30 mg/g (single measurement) (minimal confounder set from the domain library): Age, Sex, Race and ethnicity, Income-to-poverty ratio, Diabetes (self-report, medication, or HbA1c ≥6.5%), Educational attainment, Smoking status, and Body mass index. Variables on the causal pathway or sharing laboratory components with the outcome/exposure are excluded to avoid over-adjustment. Effect modification was assessed with Wald F tests of product terms (Sex, Age group). Complete-case primary analysis within the design; multiple imputation by chained equations as sensitivity analysis when covariates are missing. Prespecified sensitivity analyses: Exclude self-reported cardiovascular disease; Logistic regression (odds ratio); Multiple imputation of missing covariates (m = 10); Alternative exposure: Hypertension - JNC7 (BP ≥140/90 mm Hg or antihypertensive use); Unweighted analysis (design ignored). E-values quantified the minimum strength of unmeasured confounding needed to explain away the primary association. Two-sided P < .05 denoted significance. Analyses used R version 4.6.1 (2026-06-24) with survey 4.5, mice 3.19.0 and ggplot2 4.0.3, orchestrated by Q2E.

Results

The analytic sample included 8,187 participants (weighted to represent approximately 228.9 million US residents; 51.0% were female (weighted) and 21.3% were aged 65 years or older). Overall, hypertension - ACC/AHA 2017 (BP ≥130/80 mm hg or antihypertensive use) was associated with CKD - eGFR <60 or UACR ≥30 mg/g (single measurement) (PR, 1.57 (95% CI, 1.31-1.88)) after adjustment for age, sex, race and ethnicity, income-to-poverty ratio, diabetes (self-report, medication, or HbA1c ≥6.5%), educational attainment, smoking status, body mass index. 5 of 5 sensitivity analyses agree in direction (associations) or magnitude (prevalence).

The E-value for the primary estimate was 2.52 (CI limit 1.96); Prevalence ratio used directly.

Effect modification by sex: P = NA for interaction (Male: 2.15 (1.73-2.68); Female: 1.28 (1.04-1.57)).

Effect modification by age group: P = NA for interaction (18-39: 1.68 (1.18-2.38); 40-64: 1.78 (1.28-2.49); 65+: 1.48 (1.09-2.02)).

Discussion

In this nationally representative cross-sectional analysis, hypertension - ACC/AHA 2017 (BP ≥130/80 mm hg or antihypertensive use) was associated with CKD - eGFR <60 or UACR ≥30 mg/g (single measurement) (PR, 1.57 (95% CI, 1.31-1.88)) after adjustment for age, sex, race and ethnicity, income-to-poverty ratio, diabetes (self-report, medication, or HbA1c ≥6.5%), educational attainment, smoking status, body mass index. Strengths include a prespecified, investigator-approved protocol; guideline-versioned, citable case definitions; design-based variance estimation with appropriate examination weights; NCHS reliability standards; multiple prespecified sensitivity analyses; and a fully reproducible, provenance-tracked pipeline. Limitations: Cross-sectional design: temporality cannot be established and associations must not be interpreted causally. Single measurement of laboratory values; within-person variability and chronicity cannot be assessed. Residual confounding by unmeasured or imperfectly measured factors (e.g., diet, medications, menstrual blood loss). Self-reported comorbidities and behaviours are subject to recall and social-desirability bias. NHANES excludes institutionalized and active-duty military populations. Operational definition (KDIGO CKD): Chronicity (>3 months) cannot be confirmed from one NHANES visit.

Limitations

  • Cross-sectional design: temporality cannot be established and associations must not be interpreted causally.
  • Single measurement of laboratory values; within-person variability and chronicity cannot be assessed.
  • Residual confounding by unmeasured or imperfectly measured factors (e.g., diet, medications, menstrual blood loss).
  • Self-reported comorbidities and behaviours are subject to recall and social-desirability bias.
  • NHANES excludes institutionalized and active-duty military populations.
  • Operational definition (KDIGO CKD): Chronicity (>3 months) cannot be confirmed from one NHANES visit.
  • Operational definition (ACC/AHA 2017): BP is the mean of up to 3 readings at a single visit (oscillometric in 2017-2020/2021-2023; auscultatory in earlier cycles - mode differs across cycles).

Conclusions

In this cross-sectional study of US adults, hypertension - ACC/AHA 2017 (BP ≥130/80 mm hg or antihypertensive use) was associated with CKD - eGFR <60 or UACR ≥30 mg/g (single measurement) (PR, 1.57 (95% CI, 1.31-1.88)) after adjustment for age, sex, race and ethnicity, income-to-poverty ratio, diabetes (self-report, medication, or HbA1c ≥6.5%), educational attainment, smoking status, body mass index. This association is cross-sectional and may reflect residual confounding or reverse causation; prospective studies are needed to establish temporality.
