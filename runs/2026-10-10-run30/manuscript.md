# Association of Hypertension With Chronic Kidney Disease Among US Adults: NHANES 2017-March 2020 pre-pandemic

**Key Points**

- **Question:** Among noninstitutionalized US participants in NHANES 2017-March 2020 pre-pandemic (Age >=18 years; excluding pregnant at examination), is hypertension - ACC/AHA 2017 (BP >=130/80 mm hg or antihypertensive use) associated with a higher prevalence of CKD - eGFR <60 or UACR >=30 mg/g (single measurement), after adjustment for age, sex, race and ethnicity, income-to-poverty ratio, diabetes (self-report, medication, or HbA1c >=6.5%), educational attainment, smoking status, and body mass index?
- **Findings:** In this cross-sectional study of 8,187 participants (weighted to represent approximately 228.9 million US residents; 51.0% were female (weighted) and 21.3% were aged 65 years or older), hypertension - ACC/AHA 2017 (BP >=130/80 mm hg or antihypertensive use) was associated with CKD - eGFR <60 or UACR >=30 mg/g (single measurement) (PR, 1.57 (95% CI, 1.31-1.88)) after adjustment for age, sex, race and ethnicity, income-to-poverty ratio, diabetes (self-report, medication, or HbA1c >=6.5%), educational attainment, smoking status, body mass index.
- **Meaning:** This association is cross-sectional and may reflect residual confounding or reverse causation; prospective studies are needed to establish temporality.

Whether hypertension - ACC/AHA 2017 (BP >=130/80 mm hg or antihypertensive use) is associated with CKD - eGFR <60 or UACR >=30 mg/g (single measurement) beyond established risk factors is uncertain in nationally representative data. ## Discussion

In this nationally representative cross-sectional analysis, hypertension - ACC/AHA 2017 (BP >=130/80 mm hg or antihypertensive use) was associated with CKD - eGFR <60 or UACR >=30 mg/g (single measurement) (PR, 1.57 (95% CI, 1.31-1.88)) after adjustment for age, sex, race and ethnicity, income-to-poverty ratio, diabetes (self-report, medication, or HbA1c >=6.5%), educational attainment, smoking status, body mass index. Strengths include a prespecified, investigator-approved protocol; guideline-versioned, citable case definitions; design-based variance estimation with appropriate examination weights; NCHS reliability standards; multiple prespecified sensitivity analyses; and a fully reproducible, provenance-tracked pipeline. Limitations: Cross-sectional design: temporality cannot be established and associations must not be interpreted causally. Single measurement of laboratory values; within-person variability and chronicity cannot be assessed. Residual confounding by unmeasured or imperfectly measured factors (e.g., diet, medications, menstrual blood loss). Self-reported comorbidities and behaviours are subject to recall and social-desirability bias. NHANES excludes institutionalized and active-duty military populations. Operational definition (KDIGO CKD): Chronicity (>3 months) cannot be confirmed from one NHANES visit.

## Methods summary

- **Design:** cross-sectional analysis of the National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES), 2017-March 2020 pre-pandemic.
- **Population:** Age >=18 years; excluding pregnant at examination (analytic n = 6,701).
- **Outcome:** CKD - eGFR <60 or UACR >=30 mg/g (single measurement).
- **Exposure:** Hypertension - ACC/AHA 2017 (BP >=130/80 mm Hg or antihypertensive use).
- **Comparator:** participants without the exposure, from the same survey population.
- **Covariates:** Age, Sex, Race and ethnicity, Income-to-poverty ratio, Diabetes (self-report, medication, or HbA1c >=6.5%), Educational attainment, Smoking status, Body mass index.
- **Descriptive:** survey-weighted Table 1 by ckd - egfr <60 or uacr >=30 mg/g (single measurement); weighted prevalence with 95% CIs.
- **Model:** prevalence ratios (survey-weighted Poisson regression); sequential models: Unadjusted, Adjusted for age, sex, and race and ethnicity, Fully adjusted (primary).
- **Effect modification:** interaction tests and stratified estimates by sex, age group.
- **Survey design:** Taylor linearisation with strata and PSUs; weight WTMECPRP; design df = 25.
- **Sensitivity analyses:** 5 prespecified.
  - Exclude self-reported cardiovascular disease
  - Logistic regression (odds ratio)
  - Multiple imputation of missing covariates (m = 10)
  - Alternative exposure: Hypertension - JNC7 (BP >=140/90 mm Hg or antihypertensive use)
  - Unweighted analysis (design ignored)
- **Missing data:** complete-case primary analysis; multiple imputation as sensitivity.
- **Reporting:** STROBE; plan pre-registered by hash before data access.
- **Sample size:** Alpha 0.05 (two-sided), power 80%, target PR 1.5, DEFF 1.8: Adequately powered for the target effect (from the run's design-based standard errors). After the run: Hypertension - ACC/AHA 2017 (BP >=130/80 mm Hg or antihypertensive use): minimum detectable PR 1.29.

## Detected limitations

- **Temporality** (major): Cross-sectional: exposure and outcome are measured at the same visit, so the direction of association cannot be established.
- **Confounding** (major): Residual and unmeasured confounding cannot be excluded in an observational analysis.
- **Measurement** (moderate): Laboratory values come from a single blood or urine sample; transient abnormalities may be misclassified as persistent.
