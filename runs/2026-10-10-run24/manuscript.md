# Association of Toxoplasma Gondii vs Toxocara vs Healthy Controls With Eosinophilia Among US Adults: NHANES 2011-2012

**Key Points**

- **Question:** Among noninstitutionalized US participants in NHANES 2011-2012 (Age >=18 years), is toxoplasma gondii vs toxocara vs healthy controls associated with a higher prevalence of eosinophilia - absolute eosinophil count >=0.5 x10^9/L, after adjustment for age, sex, race and ethnicity, income-to-poverty ratio, body mass index, and born in the united states?
- **Findings:** In this cross-sectional study of 3,029 participants (weighted to represent approximately 155.1 million US residents; 50.4% were female (weighted) and 12.4% were aged 65 years or older), toxoplasma gondii vs toxocara vs healthy controls was associated with Eosinophilia - absolute eosinophil count >=0.5 x10^9/L (PR, 2.00 (95% CI, 1.16-3.47)) after adjustment for age, sex, race and ethnicity, income-to-poverty ratio, body mass index, born in the united states.
- **Meaning:** This association is cross-sectional and may reflect residual confounding or reverse causation; prospective studies are needed to establish temporality.

## Introduction

Whether toxoplasma gondii vs toxocara vs healthy controls is associated with eosinophilia - absolute eosinophil count >=0.5 x10^9/L beyond established risk factors is uncertain in nationally representative data. National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES) data permit design-based estimation that generalizes to the noninstitutionalized US population. We aimed to evaluate whether toxoplasma gondii vs toxocara vs healthy controls is associated with eosinophilia - absolute eosinophil count >=0.5 x10^9/L among US adults.

Outcome: Eosinophilia - absolute eosinophil count >=0.5 x10^9/L. Exposure: mutually exclusive groups (Healthy controls, Toxoplasma gondii only, Toxocara only, Both positive), reference 'Healthy controls'. ## Detected limitations

- **Temporality** (major): Cross-sectional: exposure and outcome are measured at the same visit, so the direction of association cannot be established.
- **Confounding** (major): Residual and unmeasured confounding cannot be excluded in an observational analysis.
- **Measurement** (moderate): Laboratory values come from a single blood or urine sample; transient abnormalities may be misclassified as persistent.
- **Comparator** (moderate): Healthy controls were screened for other diseases while exposure arms were not, which can exaggerate contrasts (healthy-control bias); an all-seronegative control group was run as a sensitivity analysis.
- **Precision** (moderate): Sample size: underpowered for the target effect (from the run's design-based standard errors) (target PR 1.5); a null result is not evidence of no association.
- **Multiplicity** (minor): 3 comparisons against the reference group; nominal P values are not multiplicity-adjusted unless stated.
- **Data** (minor): Raw values within plausible ranges: Out-of-range values: LBXSCR (1 low, 0 high); URDACT (0 low, 3 high)
