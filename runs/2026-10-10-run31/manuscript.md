# Association of Liver Stiffness With Thrombocytopenia Among US Adults: NHANES 2017-March 2020 pre-pandemic

**Key Points**

- **Question:** Among noninstitutionalized US participants in NHANES 2017-March 2020 pre-pandemic (Age >=18 years; Reliable liver elastography (complete exam, IQR/median <30%)), is greater liver stiffness (VCTE median) (per 5 kPa) associated with a higher prevalence of thrombocytopenia - platelets <150 x10^9/L, after adjustment for age, sex, race and ethnicity, income-to-poverty ratio, body mass index, diabetes (self-report, medication, or HbA1c >=6.5%), and smoking status?
- **Findings:** In this cross-sectional study of 7,463 participants (weighted to represent approximately 211.1 million US residents; 50.7% were female (weighted) and 19.4% were aged 65 years or older), liver stiffness (VCTE median) per 5 kPa was associated with Thrombocytopenia - platelets <150 x10^9/L (PR, 1.18 (95% CI, 1.13-1.24)) after adjustment for age, sex, race and ethnicity, income-to-poverty ratio, body mass index, diabetes (self-report, medication, or HbA1c >=6.5%), smoking status.
- **Meaning:** This association is cross-sectional and may reflect residual confounding or reverse causation; prospective studies are needed to establish temporality.

## Introduction

Whether liver stiffness (VCTE median) is associated with thrombocytopenia - platelets <150 x10^9/L beyond established risk factors is uncertain in nationally representative data. National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES) data permit design-based estimation that generalizes to the noninstitutionalized US population. We aimed to evaluate whether liver stiffness (VCTE median) is associated with thrombocytopenia - platelets <150 x10^9/L among US adults.

## Discussion

In this nationally representative cross-sectional analysis, liver stiffness (VCTE median) per 5 kPa was associated with Thrombocytopenia - platelets <150 x10^9/L (PR, 1.18 (95% CI, 1.13-1.24)) after adjustment for age, sex, race and ethnicity, income-to-poverty ratio, body mass index, diabetes (self-report, medication, or HbA1c >=6.5%), smoking status. Strengths include a prespecified, investigator-approved protocol; guideline-versioned, citable case definitions; design-based variance estimation with appropriate examination weights; NCHS reliability standards; multiple prespecified sensitivity analyses; and a fully reproducible, provenance-tracked pipeline. Limitations: Cross-sectional design: temporality cannot be established and associations must not be interpreted causally. Single measurement of laboratory values; within-person variability and chronicity cannot be assessed. Residual confounding by unmeasured or imperfectly measured factors (e.g., diet, medications, menstrual blood loss). Self-reported comorbidities and behaviours are subject to recall and social-desirability bias. NHANES excludes institutionalized and active-duty military populations.

## Methods summary

- **Design:** cross-sectional analysis of the National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES), 2017-March 2020 pre-pandemic.
- **Population:** Age >=18 years; Reliable liver elastography (complete exam, IQR/median <30%) (analytic n = 6,425).
- **Outcome:** Thrombocytopenia - platelets <150 x10^9/L.
- **Exposure:** Liver stiffness (VCTE median).
- **Comparator:** per-unit contrast along the exposure (lower vs higher values); quartiles as sensitivity.
- **Covariates:** Age, Sex, Race and ethnicity, Income-to-poverty ratio, Body mass index, Diabetes (self-report, medication, or HbA1c >=6.5%), Smoking status.
- **Descriptive:** survey-weighted Table 1 by thrombocytopenia - platelets <150 x10^9/l; weighted prevalence with 95% CIs.
- **Model:** prevalence ratios (survey-weighted Poisson regression); sequential models: Unadjusted, Adjusted for age, sex, and race and ethnicity, Fully adjusted (primary).
- **Effect modification:** interaction tests and stratified estimates by sex, age group.
- **Survey design:** Taylor linearisation with strata and PSUs; weight WTMECPRP; design df = 25.
- **Sensitivity analyses:** 6 prespecified.
  - Exclude heavy alcohol (>2 drinks/day men, >1 women)
  - Exclude self-reported hepatitis B or C
  - Logistic regression (odds ratio)
  - Multiple imputation of missing covariates (m = 10)
  - Exposure in weighted quartiles (Q4 vs Q1, P for trend)
  - Unweighted analysis (design ignored)
- **Missing data:** complete-case primary analysis; multiple imputation as sensitivity.
- **Reporting:** STROBE; plan pre-registered by hash before data access.
- **Sample size:** Alpha 0.05 (two-sided), power 80%, target PR 1.5, DEFF 1.8: Adequately powered for the target effect (from the run's design-based standard errors). After the run: Liver stiffness (VCTE median) (per 5 kPa): minimum detectable PR 1.06.

## Detected limitations

- **Temporality** (major): Cross-sectional: exposure and outcome are measured at the same visit, so the direction of association cannot be established.
- **Confounding** (major): Residual and unmeasured confounding cannot be excluded in an observational analysis.
- **Confounding** (moderate): E-value 1.65: a modest unmeasured confounder (RR ≈ 1.7 with both exposure and outcome) could explain the association.
- **Missing data** (minor): Complete-case loss: 13.9% of the analytic sample (1038) lack >=1 covariate. Multiple-imputation sensitivity analysis recommended.
