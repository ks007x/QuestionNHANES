Introduction

Functional iron deficiency is a common, treatable condition whose estimated burden depends on the case definition. Contemporary nationally representative estimates aligned with current guideline thresholds are needed. National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES) data permit design-based estimation that generalizes to the noninstitutionalized US population. We aimed to estimate the nationally representative prevalence of functional iron deficiency and its distribution across demographic subgroups.

Methods

Data Source

NHANES is a continuous, nationally representative survey of the US civilian noninstitutionalized population using a stratified, multistage probability design, with household interviews and standardized physical examinations and laboratory testing in mobile examination centers (MECs). We used NHANES 2017-March 2020 pre-pandemic. NHANES protocols were approved by the National Center for Health Statistics Research Ethics Review Board and participants provided written informed consent; this analysis of deidentified public-use data did not require additional institutional review board review. We followed the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) reporting guideline.

Covariates

Age; Sex; Race and ethnicity; Income-to-poverty ratio; Body mass index; Systemic inflammation (hsCRP >5 mg/L); Chronic kidney disease (eGFR <60 or UACR ≥30 mg/g).

Statistical Analysis

Prevalence estimand: survey-weighted proportions with Korn-Graubard (beta) 95% CIs, overall and by subgroup, assessed against NCHS presentation standards; population counts from weighted totals. Adjusted correlates are estimated with survey-weighted quasi-Poisson regression (prevalence ratios). All analyses incorporated the WTMECPRP sample weight (mec component), masked variance pseudo-strata and pseudo-PSUs (Taylor series linearization; 24 strata, 49 PSUs, design df = 25); subpopulations were analyzed with domain (subset) estimation to preserve the full design. Proportions use Korn-Graubard 95% CIs and were evaluated against the NCHS Data Presentation Standards for Proportions. Regression inference used t distributions with design degrees of freedom. Correlates model includes Age, Sex, Race and ethnicity, Income-to-poverty ratio, Body mass index, Systemic inflammation (hsCRP >5 mg/L), and Chronic kidney disease (eGFR <60 or UACR ≥30 mg/g) to describe independent associations with functional iron deficiency - ferritin ≥30 ng/mL with TSAT <20%; these are descriptive, not causal, contrasts. Variables on the causal pathway or sharing laboratory components with the outcome/exposure are excluded to avoid over-adjustment. Complete-case primary analysis within the design; multiple imputation by chained equations as sensitivity analysis when covariates are missing. Prespecified sensitivity analyses: Alternative outcome definition: ESC HF; Exclude hsCRP >5 mg/L (inflammation raises ferritin); Exclude participants treated for anemia; Exclude pregnant participants; Unweighted analysis (design ignored). Two-sided P < .05 denoted significance. Analyses used R version 4.6.1 (2026-06-24) with survey 4.5, mice 3.19.0 and ggplot2 4.0.3, orchestrated by Q2E.

Human Oversight and Use of AI

The protocol was drafted by Q2E research agents (offline mode: rule-based agents only), then reviewed, edited and approved by the investigator before execution. Statistical code was not generated free-form: analyses ran in a versioned R engine whose derived-variable expressions pass a whitelisted parser. Results

The analytic sample included 8,212 participants (weighted to represent approximately 231.3 million US residents; 51.7% were female (weighted) and 20.4% were aged 65 years or older). Overall, the weighted prevalence of functional iron deficiency was 15.4% (95% CI, 14.4%-16.5%), corresponding to approximately 35.7 million people. Prevalence ranged from 11.8% (sex: Male) to 20.5% (race and ethnicity: Other or multiracial). 4 of 4 sensitivity analyses agree in direction (associations) or magnitude (prevalence). The estimate was consistent with the published estimate of 15% (Tawfik et al. 2024, US adults ≥18 y, NHANES 2017-March 2020).

Discussion

In this nationally representative cross-sectional analysis, the weighted prevalence of functional iron deficiency was 15.4% (95% CI, 14.4%-16.5%), corresponding to approximately 35.7 million people. The estimate was consistent with the published estimate of 15% (Tawfik et al. 2024, US adults ≥18 y, NHANES 2017-March 2020). Strengths include a prespecified, investigator-approved protocol; guideline-versioned, citable case definitions; design-based variance estimation with appropriate examination weights; NCHS reliability standards; multiple prespecified sensitivity analyses; and a fully reproducible, provenance-tracked pipeline. Limitations: Cross-sectional design: temporality cannot be established and associations must not be interpreted causally. Single measurement of laboratory values; within-person variability and chronicity cannot be assessed. Residual confounding by unmeasured or imperfectly measured factors (e.g., diet, medications, menstrual blood loss). Self-reported comorbidities and behaviours are subject to recall and social-desirability bias. NHANES excludes institutionalized and active-duty military populations. Operational definition (Ferritin ≥30 & TSAT <20%): TSAT uses the NHANES-reported value (LBDPCT); if absent it is computed as 100 x serum iron / TIBC.

Limitations

  • Cross-sectional design: temporality cannot be established and associations must not be interpreted causally.

  • Operational definition (Ferritin ≥30 & TSAT <20%): TSAT uses the NHANES-reported value (LBDPCT); if absent it is computed as 100 x serum iron / TIBC.

Conclusions

In this cross-sectional study of US adults, the weighted prevalence of functional iron deficiency was 15.4% (95% CI, 14.4%-16.5%), corresponding to approximately 35.7 million people. Functional iron deficiency affects a substantial share of this population under current guideline-based definitions; case definitions materially change prevalence, so the definition should be reported explicitly.
