Introduction

Parasitic infection (measured serologies) is a common, treatable condition whose estimated burden depends on the case definition. Contemporary nationally representative estimates aligned with current guideline thresholds are needed. National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES) data permit design-based estimation that generalizes to the noninstitutionalized US population. We aimed to estimate the nationally representative prevalence of parasitic infection (measured serologies) and its distribution across demographic subgroups.

Methods

Data Source

NHANES is a continuous, nationally representative survey of the US civilian noninstitutionalized population using a stratified, multistage probability design, with household interviews and standardized physical examinations and laboratory testing in mobile examination centers (MECs). We used NHANES 2011-2012. NHANES protocols were approved by the National Center for Health Statistics Research Ethics Review Board and participants provided written informed consent; this analysis of deidentified public-use data did not require additional institutional review board review. We followed the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) reporting guideline.

Definitions

[verified] Operationalization: Surplus-specimen subsample (participants aged 6+ with stored sera): analysed with the surplus-specimen weights (WTSSTT2Y), not the full exam weights. [verified] Operationalization: Assay and cut-off differ by cycle; do not pool 1999-2004 with 2011-2012 without a sensitivity analysis by period. 1999-2004 testing was limited to ages 6-49 years. 2011-2012 is a surplus-specimen subsample analysed with WTSSTT2Y weights. 1999-2000 codebook does not state the cut-off; ≥10 IU/mL is assumed from the 2001-2004 documentation. Covariates

Age; Sex; Race and ethnicity; Income-to-poverty ratio; Born in the United States.

Statistical Analysis

Prevalence estimand: survey-weighted proportions with Korn-Graubard (beta) 95% CIs, overall and by subgroup, assessed against NCHS presentation standards; population counts from weighted totals. Adjusted correlates are estimated with survey-weighted quasi-Poisson regression (prevalence ratios). All analyses incorporated the WTSSTT2Y sample weight (subsample component), masked variance pseudo-strata and pseudo-PSUs (Taylor series linearization; 14 strata, 31 PSUs, design df = 17); subpopulations were analyzed with domain (subset) estimation to preserve the full design. Proportions use Korn-Graubard 95% CIs and were evaluated against the NCHS Data Presentation Standards for Proportions. Regression inference used t distributions with design degrees of freedom. Correlates model includes Age, Sex, Race and ethnicity, Income-to-poverty ratio, and Born in the United States to describe independent associations with any measured parasite seropositivity - toxocara or toxoplasma IgG (2011-2012); these are descriptive, not causal, contrasts. Variables on the causal pathway or sharing laboratory components with the outcome/exposure are excluded to avoid over-adjustment. Complete-case primary analysis within the design; multiple imputation by chained equations as sensitivity analysis when covariates are missing. Prespecified sensitivity analyses: Unweighted analysis (design ignored). Two-sided P < .05 denoted significance. Analyses used R version 4.6.1 (2026-06-24) with survey 4.5, mice 3.19.0 and ggplot2 4.0.3, orchestrated by Q2E.

Human Oversight and Use of AI

The protocol was drafted by Q2E research agents (offline mode: rule-based agents only), then reviewed, edited and approved by the investigator before execution. No participant-level data were sent to any language model. Statistical code was not generated free-form: analyses ran in a versioned R engine whose derived-variable expressions pass a whitelisted parser. All outputs were checked by 19 automated validation rules and signed off by the investigator.

Results

The analytic sample included 4,588 participants (weighted to represent approximately 232.4 million US residents; 51.7% were female (weighted) and 16.9% were aged 65 years or older). Overall, the weighted prevalence of parasitic infection (measured serologies) was 16.9% (95% CI, 14.8%-19.3%), corresponding to approximately 39.4 million people. Prevalence ranged from 11.8% (race and ethnicity: Other or multiracial) to 35.1% (born in the united states: No). 1 of 1 sensitivity analyses agree in direction (associations) or magnitude (prevalence).

Discussion

In this nationally representative cross-sectional analysis, the weighted prevalence of parasitic infection (measured serologies) was 16.9% (95% CI, 14.8%-19.3%), corresponding to approximately 39.4 million people. Strengths include a prespecified, investigator-approved protocol; guideline-versioned, citable case definitions; design-based variance estimation with appropriate examination weights; NCHS reliability standards; multiple prespecified sensitivity analyses; and a fully reproducible, provenance-tracked pipeline. Limitations: Cross-sectional design: temporality cannot be established and associations must not be interpreted causally. Single measurement of laboratory values; within-person variability and chronicity cannot be assessed. Residual confounding by unmeasured or imperfectly measured factors (e.g., diet, medications, menstrual blood loss). Self-reported comorbidities and behaviours are subject to recall and social-desirability bias. NHANES excludes institutionalized and active-duty military populations. Operational definition (Toxocara or T. gondii IgG+): NHANES measures only selected parasites by serology (Toxocara, Toxoplasma; Trichomonas and Cryptosporidium in limited cycles). Intestinal helminths, stool ova and parasites, malaria and Strongyloides are not measured.

Limitations

  • Cross-sectional design: temporality cannot be established and associations must not be interpreted causally.
  • Single measurement of laboratory values; within-person variability and chronicity cannot be assessed.
  • Residual confounding by unmeasured or imperfectly measured factors (e.g., diet, medications, menstrual blood loss).
  • Self-reported comorbidities and behaviours are subject to recall and social-desirability bias.
  • NHANES excludes institutionalized and active-duty military populations.
  • Operational definition (Toxocara or T. gondii IgG+): NHANES measures only selected parasites by serology (Toxocara, Toxoplasma; Trichomonas and Cryptosporidium in limited cycles). Intestinal helminths, stool ova and parasites, malaria and Strongyloides are not measured.
  • Operational definition (Toxocara or T. gondii IgG+): Composite is defined only where both tests were run on the same specimens (2011-2012).

Conclusions

In this cross-sectional study of US adults, the weighted prevalence of parasitic infection (measured serologies) was 16.9% (95% CI, 14.8%-19.3%), corresponding to approximately 39.4 million people. Parasitic infection (measured serologies) affects a substantial share of this population under current guideline-based definitions; case definitions materially change prevalence, so the definition should be reported explicitly.
