# Prevalence of Parasitic Infection (Measured Serologies) Among US Adults: A Cross-Sectional Analysis of NHANES 2011-2012

**Key Points**

- **Question:** Among noninstitutionalized US participants in NHANES 2011-2012 (Age >=18 years), what is the survey-weighted prevalence of parasitic infection (measured serologies) (positive for Toxocara (SSTCAR = 1) or Toxoplasma IgG (>=33 IU/mL) in the 2011-2012 surplus-specimen subsample), overall and by sex, age group, race and ethnicity, and born in the united states?
- **Findings:** In this cross-sectional study of 4,588 participants (weighted to represent approximately 232.4 million US residents; 51.7% were female (weighted) and 16.9% were aged 65 years or older), the weighted prevalence of parasitic infection (measured serologies) was 16.9% (95% CI, 14.8%-19.3%), corresponding to approximately 39.4 million people.
- **Meaning:** Parasitic infection (measured serologies) affects a substantial share of this population under current guideline-based definitions; case definitions materially change prevalence, so the definition should be reported explicitly.

## Introduction

Parasitic infection (measured serologies) is a common, treatable condition whose estimated burden depends on the case definition. Contemporary nationally representative estimates aligned with current guideline thresholds are needed. National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES) data permit design-based estimation that generalizes to the noninstitutionalized US population. We aimed to estimate the nationally representative prevalence of parasitic infection (measured serologies) and its distribution across demographic subgroups.

## Discussion

In this nationally representative cross-sectional analysis, the weighted prevalence of parasitic infection (measured serologies) was 16.9% (95% CI, 14.8%-19.3%), corresponding to approximately 39.4 million people. Strengths include a prespecified, investigator-approved protocol; guideline-versioned, citable case definitions; design-based variance estimation with appropriate examination weights; NCHS reliability standards; multiple prespecified sensitivity analyses; and a fully reproducible, provenance-tracked pipeline. Limitations: Cross-sectional design: temporality cannot be established and associations must not be interpreted causally. Single measurement of laboratory values; within-person variability and chronicity cannot be assessed. Residual confounding by unmeasured or imperfectly measured factors (e.g., diet, medications, menstrual blood loss). Self-reported comorbidities and behaviours are subject to recall and social-desirability bias. NHANES excludes institutionalized and active-duty military populations. Operational definition (Toxocara or T. gondii IgG+): NHANES measures only selected parasites by serology (Toxocara, Toxoplasma; Trichomonas and Cryptosporidium in limited cycles). Intestinal helminths, stool ova and parasites, malaria and Strongyloides are not measured.

## Limitations

- Operational definition (Toxocara or T. gondii IgG+): Composite is defined only where both tests were run on the same specimens (2011-2012).
