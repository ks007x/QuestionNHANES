Introduction

Whether toxoplasma gondii vs toxocara vs healthy controls is associated with eosinophilia - absolute eosinophil count ≥0.5 x10^9/L beyond established risk factors is uncertain in nationally representative data. National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES) data permit design-based estimation that generalizes to the noninstitutionalized US population. We aimed to evaluate whether toxoplasma gondii vs toxocara vs healthy controls is associated with eosinophilia - absolute eosinophil count ≥0.5 x10^9/L among US adults.

Methods

Data Source

NHANES is a continuous, nationally representative survey of the US civilian noninstitutionalized population using a stratified, multistage probability design, with household interviews and standardized physical examinations and laboratory testing in mobile examination centers (MECs). We used NHANES 2011-2012. NHANES protocols were approved by the National Center for Health Statistics Research Ethics Review Board and participants provided written informed consent; this analysis of deidentified public-use data did not require additional institutional review board review. We followed the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) reporting guideline.

Statistical Analysis

Binary outcome: survey-weighted quasi-Poisson regression with log link estimates prevalence ratios (PRs). PRs are preferred to odds ratios for cross-sectional data because ORs exaggerate associations when the outcome is common (>10%); the quasi-Poisson variance is robust under the design-based (Taylor linearization) estimator. Logistic regression is run as a sensitivity analysis. All analyses incorporated the WTSSTT2Y sample weight (subsample component), masked variance pseudo-strata and pseudo-PSUs (Taylor series linearization; 14 strata, 31 PSUs, design df = 17); subpopulations were analyzed with domain (subset) estimation to preserve the full design. Proportions use Korn-Graubard 95% CIs and were evaluated against the NCHS Data Presentation Standards for Proportions. Regression inference used t distributions with design degrees of freedom. Covariates were selected as plausible common causes of toxoplasma gondii vs toxocara vs healthy controls and eosinophilia - absolute eosinophil count ≥0.5 x10^9/L (minimal confounder set from the domain library): Age, Sex, Race and ethnicity, Income-to-poverty ratio, Body mass index, and Born in the United States. Variables on the causal pathway or sharing laboratory components with the outcome/exposure are excluded to avoid over-adjustment. Effect modification was assessed with Wald F tests of product terms (Sex, Age group). Complete-case primary analysis within the design; multiple imputation by chained equations as sensitivity analysis when covariates are missing. Prespecified sensitivity analyses: Alternative outcome definition: AEC ≥1.5; Logistic regression (odds ratio); Multiple imputation of missing covariates (m = 10); Alternative control group: everyone negative for all groups, no health screening (healthy-control bias check); Unweighted analysis (design ignored). E-values quantified the minimum strength of unmeasured confounding needed to explain away the primary association. Two-sided P < .05 denoted significance. Analyses used R version 4.6.1 (2026-06-24) with survey 4.5, mice 3.19.0 and ggplot2 4.0.3, orchestrated by Q2E.

Human Oversight and Use of AI

The protocol was drafted by Q2E research agents (offline mode: rule-based agents only), then reviewed, edited and approved by the investigator before execution. No participant-level data were sent to any language model. Statistical code was not generated free-form: analyses ran in a versioned R engine whose derived-variable expressions pass a whitelisted parser. All outputs were checked by 20 automated validation rules and signed off by the investigator.

Results

The analytic sample included 3,029 participants (weighted to represent approximately 155.1 million US residents; 50.4% were female (weighted) and 12.4% were aged 65 years or older). Overall, toxoplasma gondii vs toxocara vs healthy controls was associated with Eosinophilia - absolute eosinophil count ≥0.5 x10^9/L (PR, 2.00 (95% CI, 1.16-3.47)) after adjustment for age, sex, race and ethnicity, income-to-poverty ratio, body mass index, born in the united states. 4 of 5 sensitivity analyses agree in direction (associations) or magnitude (prevalence). Divergent: Alternative outcome definition: AEC ≥1.5

The E-value for the primary estimate was 3.42 (CI limit 1.59); Prevalence ratio used directly.

Effect modification by sex: P = .51 for interaction (Male: 1.33 (0.75-2.36); Female: 4.19 (1.71-10.27)).

Effect modification by age group: P = .03 for interaction (18-39: 1.71 (0.46-6.36); 40-64: 1.52 (0.37-6.22); 65+: 2.58 (0.44-15.11)).

Discussion

In this nationally representative cross-sectional analysis, toxoplasma gondii vs toxocara vs healthy controls was associated with Eosinophilia - absolute eosinophil count ≥0.5 x10^9/L (PR, 2.00 (95% CI, 1.16-3.47)) after adjustment for age, sex, race and ethnicity, income-to-poverty ratio, body mass index, born in the united states. Strengths include a prespecified, investigator-approved protocol; guideline-versioned, citable case definitions; design-based variance estimation with appropriate examination weights; NCHS reliability standards; multiple prespecified sensitivity analyses; and a fully reproducible, provenance-tracked pipeline. Limitations: Cross-sectional design: temporality cannot be established and associations must not be interpreted causally. Single measurement of laboratory values; within-person variability and chronicity cannot be assessed. Residual confounding by unmeasured or imperfectly measured factors (e.g., diet, medications, menstrual blood loss). Self-reported comorbidities and behaviours are subject to recall and social-desirability bias. NHANES excludes institutionalized and active-duty military populations.
