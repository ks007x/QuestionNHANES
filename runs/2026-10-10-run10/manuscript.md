# Association of Physical Activity With Diabetes Among US Adults: NHANES 2017-March 2020 pre-pandemic

**Key Points**

- **Question:** Among noninstitutionalized US participants in NHANES 2017-March 2020 pre-pandemic (Age >=18 years; excluding pregnant at examination), is any vigorous recreational activity associated with a higher prevalence of diabetes - self-report, medication, or HbA1c >=6.5%, after adjustment for age, sex, race and ethnicity, income-to-poverty ratio, educational attainment, smoking status, and body mass index?
- **Findings:** In this cross-sectional study of 8,878 participants (weighted to represent approximately 245.5 million US residents; 51.3% were female (weighted) and 20.7% were aged 65 years or older), any vigorous recreational activity was associated with Diabetes - self-report, medication, or HbA1c >=6.5% (PR, 0.59 (95% CI, 0.46-0.75)) after adjustment for age, sex, race and ethnicity, income-to-poverty ratio, educational attainment, smoking status, body mass index.
- **Meaning:** This association is cross-sectional and may reflect residual confounding or reverse causation; prospective studies are needed to establish temporality.

## Introduction

Whether any vigorous recreational activity is associated with diabetes - self-report, medication, or HbA1c >=6.5% beyond established risk factors is uncertain in nationally representative data. National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES) data permit design-based estimation that generalizes to the noninstitutionalized US population. We aimed to evaluate whether any vigorous recreational activity is associated with diabetes - self-report, medication, or HbA1c >=6.5% among US adults.

## Discussion

In this nationally representative cross-sectional analysis, any vigorous recreational activity was associated with Diabetes - self-report, medication, or HbA1c >=6.5% (PR, 0.59 (95% CI, 0.46-0.75)) after adjustment for age, sex, race and ethnicity, income-to-poverty ratio, educational attainment, smoking status, body mass index. Strengths include a prespecified, investigator-approved protocol; guideline-versioned, citable case definitions; design-based variance estimation with appropriate examination weights; NCHS reliability standards; multiple prespecified sensitivity analyses; and a fully reproducible, provenance-tracked pipeline. Limitations: Cross-sectional design: temporality cannot be established and associations must not be interpreted causally. Single measurement of laboratory values; within-person variability and chronicity cannot be assessed. Residual confounding by unmeasured or imperfectly measured factors (e.g., diet, medications, menstrual blood loss). Self-reported comorbidities and behaviours are subject to recall and social-desirability bias. NHANES excludes institutionalized and active-duty military populations. Operational definition (ADA (HbA1c)): Fasting glucose omitted so the full MEC sample/weight can be used; add FPG >=126 mg/dL in a fasting-subsample sensitivity analysis.

## Design audit (Defensible with caveats)

- **CAUTION** Outcome: Outcome is partly defined by treatment. Treated people count as cases. If the exposure affects access to care, outcome misclassification will differ by exposure. Report the measured-only definition as a sensitivity analysis. _(addresses: Outcome misclassification)_
- **CAUTION** Rationale: Run the PubMed/MEDLINE novelty check. A stated hypothesis exists; checking prior NHANES work documents the a priori rationale and avoids re-running a crowded exposure-outcome pair. _(addresses: Template exposure-outcome pairs without rationale)_
- **CAUTION** Oversight: Add a named statistician or epidemiologist sign-off. Investigator approval is required to run; journals increasingly expect documented methodological co-authorship for large-database studies. _(addresses: Data access without methodological expertise)_
- **PASS** Comparator: Explicit, same-population comparator. Contrast: participants in the same eligible population without any vigorous recreational activity. Both groups come from the same probability sample and eligibility criteria (no convenience controls). _(addresses: Inappropriate comparator)_
- **PASS** Outcome: Outcome includes measured (exam or laboratory) data. Objective measurement captures undiagnosed cases, unlike code-based EHR outcomes. _(addresses: Code-based / unvalidated outcome)_
- **PASS** Temporality: Reverse causation: diagnosed people may have changed behaviour. People who know their diagnosis often change diet, activity or smoking (sick-quitter effect), which can reverse the apparent association. Sensitivity analysis excluding diagnosed participants is planned. _(addresses: Temporality / reverse causation)_
- **PASS** Confounding: Prespecified adjustment set. Adjusted for 7 covariates. Mediators and definitional overlaps are removed automatically, and an E-value quantifies how strong unmeasured confounding would need to be. _(addresses: Residual confounding (only platform-captured variables))_
- **PASS** Prespecification: Protocol hash-locked before results. SHA-256 of the analysis plan is recorded at approval; any later edit voids the approval. All 6 prespecified sensitivity analyses are reported regardless of result; 5 subgroup/interaction tests are labelled exploratory. _(addresses: Post-hoc analyses / p-hacking)_
- **PASS** Reproducibility: Executable methods with data provenance. Every result comes with the compiled plan, R code, reproduce.R and MD5 checksums of the exact CDC files - the methods section describes what actually ran. _(addresses: Methods the platform cannot actually run)_
- **PASS** Meaningfulness: Effect size interpretable. PR 0.59 (0.46-0.75). Report absolute prevalence differences alongside the ratio. _(addresses: Significance without clinical meaning)_
