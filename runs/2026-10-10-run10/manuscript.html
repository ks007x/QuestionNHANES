Introduction

Whether any vigorous recreational activity is associated with diabetes - self-report, medication, or HbA1c ≥6.5% beyond established risk factors is uncertain in nationally representative data. National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES) data permit design-based estimation that generalizes to the noninstitutionalized US population. We aimed to evaluate whether any vigorous recreational activity is associated with diabetes - self-report, medication, or HbA1c ≥6.5% among US adults.

Methods

Data Source

NHANES is a continuous, nationally representative survey of the US civilian noninstitutionalized population using a stratified, multistage probability design, with household interviews and standardized physical examinations and laboratory testing in mobile examination centers (MECs). We used NHANES 2017-March 2020 pre-pandemic. NHANES protocols were approved by the National Center for Health Statistics Research Ethics Review Board and participants provided written informed consent; this analysis of deidentified public-use data did not require additional institutional review board review. We followed the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) reporting guideline.

Statistical Analysis

Binary outcome: survey-weighted quasi-Poisson regression with log link estimates prevalence ratios (PRs). PRs are preferred to odds ratios for cross-sectional data because ORs exaggerate associations when the outcome is common (>10%); the quasi-Poisson variance is robust under the design-based (Taylor linearization) estimator. Logistic regression is run as a sensitivity analysis. All analyses incorporated the WTMECPRP sample weight (mec component), masked variance pseudo-strata and pseudo-PSUs (Taylor series linearization; 24 strata, 49 PSUs, design df = 25); subpopulations were analyzed with domain (subset) estimation to preserve the full design. Proportions use Korn-Graubard 95% CIs and were evaluated against the NCHS Data Presentation Standards for Proportions. Regression inference used t distributions with design degrees of freedom. Covariates were selected as plausible common causes of any vigorous recreational activity and diabetes - self-report, medication, or HbA1c ≥6.5% (minimal confounder set from the domain library): Age, Sex, Race and ethnicity, Income-to-poverty ratio, Educational attainment, Smoking status, and Body mass index. Variables on the causal pathway or sharing laboratory components with the outcome/exposure are excluded to avoid over-adjustment. Effect modification was assessed with Wald F tests of product terms (Sex, Age group). Complete-case primary analysis within the design; multiple imputation by chained equations as sensitivity analysis when covariates are missing. Prespecified sensitivity analyses: Exclude participants on glucose-lowering therapy; Exclude anemia (HbA1c unreliable); Logistic regression (odds ratio); Multiple imputation of missing covariates (m = 10); Unweighted analysis (design ignored); Exclude participants aware of the diagnosis (reverse causation). E-values quantified the minimum strength of unmeasured confounding needed to explain away the primary association. Two-sided P < .05 denoted significance. Analyses used R version 4.6.1 (2026-06-24) with survey 4.5, mice 3.19.0 and ggplot2 4.0.3, orchestrated by Q2E.

Results

The analytic sample included 8,878 participants (weighted to represent approximately 245.5 million US residents; 51.3% were female (weighted) and 20.7% were aged 65 years or older). Overall, any vigorous recreational activity was associated with Diabetes - self-report, medication, or HbA1c ≥6.5% (PR, 0.59 (95% CI, 0.46-0.75)) after adjustment for age, sex, race and ethnicity, income-to-poverty ratio, educational attainment, smoking status, body mass index. 6 of 6 sensitivity analyses agree in direction (associations) or magnitude (prevalence).

The E-value for the primary estimate was 2.78 (CI limit 1.99); Prevalence ratio used directly.

Effect modification by sex: P = .68 for interaction (Male: 0.63 (0.45-0.89); Female: 0.53 (0.34-0.84)).

Effect modification by age group: P = .003 for interaction (18-39: 0.70 (0.34-1.45); 40-64: 0.77 (0.55-1.08); 65+: 0.26 (0.18-0.37)).

Discussion

In this nationally representative cross-sectional analysis, any vigorous recreational activity was associated with Diabetes - self-report, medication, or HbA1c ≥6.5% (PR, 0.59 (95% CI, 0.46-0.75)) after adjustment for age, sex, race and ethnicity, income-to-poverty ratio, educational attainment, smoking status, body mass index. Strengths include a prespecified, investigator-approved protocol; guideline-versioned, citable case definitions; design-based variance estimation with appropriate examination weights; NCHS reliability standards; multiple prespecified sensitivity analyses; and a fully reproducible, provenance-tracked pipeline. Limitations: Cross-sectional design: temporality cannot be established and associations must not be interpreted causally. Single measurement of laboratory values; within-person variability and chronicity cannot be assessed. Residual confounding by unmeasured or imperfectly measured factors (e.g., diet, medications, menstrual blood loss). Self-reported comorbidities and behaviours are subject to recall and social-desirability bias. NHANES excludes institutionalized and active-duty military populations. Operational definition (ADA (HbA1c)): Fasting glucose omitted so the full MEC sample/weight can be used; add FPG ≥126 mg/dL in a fasting-subsample sensitivity analysis.
