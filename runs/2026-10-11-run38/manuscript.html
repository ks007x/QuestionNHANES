Introduction

Whether liver stiffness (VCTE median) is associated with thrombocytopenia - platelets <150 x10^9/L beyond established risk factors is uncertain in nationally representative data. National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES) data permit design-based estimation that generalizes to the noninstitutionalized US population. We aimed to evaluate whether liver stiffness (VCTE median) is associated with thrombocytopenia - platelets <150 x10^9/L among US adults.

Methods

Data Source

NHANES is a continuous, nationally representative survey of the US civilian noninstitutionalized population using a stratified, multistage probability design, with household interviews and standardized physical examinations and laboratory testing in mobile examination centers (MECs). We used NHANES 2017-March 2020 pre-pandemic. NHANES protocols were approved by the National Center for Health Statistics Research Ethics Review Board and participants provided written informed consent; this analysis of deidentified public-use data did not require additional institutional review board review. We followed the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) reporting guideline.

  2. 14,300 Examined in the MEC (positive MEC weight) (excluded 1,260)
  3. 8,965 Age ≥18 years (excluded 5,335)
  4. 8,510 Non-missing outcome (Thrombocytopenia - platelets <150 x10^9/L) (excluded 455)

Definitions

  • Thrombocytopenia - platelets <150 x10^9/L. Platelet count <150 x10^9/L. Source: Conventional laboratory definition. [verified] plt < 150
  • Liver stiffness (VCTE median) (kPa): LUXSMED

Covariates

Age; Sex; Race and ethnicity; Income-to-poverty ratio; Body mass index; Diabetes (self-report, medication, or HbA1c ≥6.5%); Smoking status.

Statistical Analysis

Binary outcome: survey-weighted quasi-Poisson regression with log link estimates prevalence ratios (PRs). PRs are preferred to odds ratios for cross-sectional data because ORs exaggerate associations when the outcome is common (>10%); the quasi-Poisson variance is robust under the design-based (Taylor linearization) estimator. Logistic regression is run as a sensitivity analysis. All analyses incorporated the WTMECPRP sample weight (mec component), masked variance pseudo-strata and pseudo-PSUs (Taylor series linearization; 24 strata, 49 PSUs, design df = 25); subpopulations were analyzed with domain (subset) estimation to preserve the full design. Proportions use Korn-Graubard 95% CIs and were evaluated against the NCHS Data Presentation Standards for Proportions. Regression inference used t distributions with design degrees of freedom. Covariates were selected as plausible common causes of liver stiffness (VCTE median) and thrombocytopenia - platelets <150 x10^9/L (minimal confounder set from the domain library): Age, Sex, Race and ethnicity, Income-to-poverty ratio, Body mass index, Diabetes (self-report, medication, or HbA1c ≥6.5%), and Smoking status. Variables on the causal pathway or sharing laboratory components with the outcome/exposure are excluded to avoid over-adjustment. Effect modification was assessed with Wald F tests of product terms (Sex, Age group). Complete-case primary analysis within the design; multiple imputation by chained equations as sensitivity analysis when covariates are missing. Prespecified sensitivity analyses: Exclude heavy alcohol (>2 drinks/day men, >1 women); Exclude self-reported hepatitis B or C; Logistic regression (odds ratio); Multiple imputation of missing covariates (m = 10); Exposure in weighted quartiles (Q4 vs Q1, P for trend); Unweighted analysis (design ignored). E-values quantified the minimum strength of unmeasured confounding needed to explain away the primary association. Two-sided P < .05 denoted significance. Analyses used R version 4.6.1 (2026-06-24) with survey 4.5, mice 3.19.0 and ggplot2 4.0.3, orchestrated by Q2E.

Results

The analytic sample included 7,978 participants (weighted to represent approximately 225.3 million US residents; 51.0% were female (weighted) and 19.7% were aged 65 years or older). Overall, liver stiffness (VCTE median) per 5 kPa was associated with Thrombocytopenia - platelets <150 x10^9/L (PR, 1.19 (95% CI, 1.16-1.23)) after adjustment for age, sex, race and ethnicity, income-to-poverty ratio, body mass index, diabetes (self-report, medication, or HbA1c ≥6.5%), smoking status. 6 of 6 sensitivity analyses agree in direction (associations) or magnitude (prevalence).

The E-value for the primary estimate was 1.68 (CI limit 1.58); Prevalence ratio used directly.

Effect modification by sex: P = .26 for interaction (Male: 1.18 (1.14-1.23); Female: 1.28 (1.18-1.38)).

Effect modification by age group: P = .03 for interaction (18-39: 0.29 (0.07-1.31); 40-64: 1.24 (1.17-1.31); 65+: 1.17 (1.10-1.23)).

Discussion

In this nationally representative cross-sectional analysis, liver stiffness (VCTE median) per 5 kPa was associated with Thrombocytopenia - platelets <150 x10^9/L (PR, 1.19 (95% CI, 1.16-1.23)) after adjustment for age, sex, race and ethnicity, income-to-poverty ratio, body mass index, diabetes (self-report, medication, or HbA1c ≥6.5%), smoking status. Strengths include a prespecified, investigator-approved protocol; guideline-versioned, citable case definitions; design-based variance estimation with appropriate examination weights; NCHS reliability standards; multiple prespecified sensitivity analyses; and a fully reproducible, provenance-tracked pipeline. Limitations: Cross-sectional design: temporality cannot be established and associations must not be interpreted causally. Single measurement of laboratory values; within-person variability and chronicity cannot be assessed. Residual confounding by unmeasured or imperfectly measured factors (e.g., diet, medications, menstrual blood loss). Self-reported comorbidities and behaviours are subject to recall and social-desirability bias. NHANES excludes institutionalized and active-duty military populations.

Conclusions
